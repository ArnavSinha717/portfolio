Ripple-Aware Collision Avoidance for Satellite Mega-Constellations

Research. Paper accepted at ICon INDIA 2026 (IEEE technically co-sponsored).

In 620,275 real Space-Track orbit records (1,800 Starlink satellites, 11,906 detected manoeuvres), each manoeuvre left a satellite’s public catalogue entry 2.2× less accurate, producing 120 false and 111 missed collision alerts against 136 genuine ones in screening.

Built a GPU JAX orbital simulator (two-body + J2, 837 real objects, 14.4M object-steps/s on a laptop GPU): announcing burns at planning time removed 92% of induced false alarms. A 103K-parameter graph-neural-network policy trained with MAPPO and a Lagrangian safety constraint left 11% fewer close approaches unresolved when given fresh data (paired t = 4.67, 8/8 held-out worlds).

RAG over 48 legal contracts with MongoDB Atlas Vector Search, Gemini embeddings and a BGE cross-encoder reranker. A 9-configuration retrieval study (189 questions, bootstrap 95% CIs) picked dense + rerank at recall@5 0.815 vs 0.487 for BM25; HyDE, hybrid and multi-query added latency without significant gains. Research

Accepted ICon INDIA 2026

R. Bansal, S. Madan, A. Sinha, Karthik G M, “Ripple-Aware Cooperative Collision Avoidance for Satellite Mega-Constellations,” accepted at ICon INDIA 2026 (IEEE technically co-sponsored), Pondicherry University, Nov 2026.

Every time a satellite manoeuvres, whether raising its orbit or dodging a collision, its entry in the public orbit catalogue becomes less accurate, and every operator screening against that catalogue inherits the error as false or missed alerts. We measured this ripple effect in real Starlink data, then showed in simulation that announcing burns at planning time removes most of the false alarms it causes, and that a cooperative multi-agent policy leaves fewer close approaches unresolved when given fresh data.
